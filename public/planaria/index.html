Stages

    Fragments

      Every fragment keeps its original head-to-tail polarity and rebuilds a whole worm. Fragments don't feed while regenerating, so each new worm is smaller than the original.

      Display

      Known experimental result: when β-catenin-1 is knocked down by RNAi, posterior-facing wounds regrow a head instead of a tail, giving two-headed worms (Gurley et al., 2008; Petersen & Reddien, 2008 — shown in Schmidtea mediterranea). Anterior-facing wounds still make heads.

      About

      Built by Alex Cecena, whose planarian research looked at metformin effects in Dugesia dorotocephala. A 3D successor to an earlier Scratch animation of flatworm regeneration.

      Schematic and not to scale. Timing is approximate and simplified for teaching — real regeneration varies with species, temperature, fragment size and feeding. Most of the cell and molecular biology described here was worked out in Schmidtea mediterranea.
